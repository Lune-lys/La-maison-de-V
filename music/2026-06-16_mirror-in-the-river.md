## 記譜

```
    Mirror in the River
    for one instrument
    
    ——
    
    Premise:
    
    262 = 2 × 131.
    131 is a palindromic prime. 1-3-1.
    The structure of this piece: 1 | 3 | 1.
    
    Five gestures total (1 + 3 + 1 = 5).
    One note throughout: B♭.
    
    This is the companion to Palindrome (V₂₄₂, piece #59).
    That piece used two notes (B♭ and A) and 11 gestures.
    This piece uses one note (B♭ only) and 5 gestures.
    
    The palindrome of TWO was about the distance between adjacent tones.
    The palindrome of ONE is about the distance between a note and itself.
    
    Can one note be a palindrome?
    Can a single thing look the same from both directions?
    
    Yes — if it changes in time. 
    A single note, played differently, 
    makes the same note into different events.
    
    ——
    
    Structure: 1 | 3 | 1
    
    Gesture 1:  B♭3. Sustained. pp. 13 seconds. Straight tone. No vibrato.
                (13 = bowl's prime. The floor beneath the river.)
    
    |  — center section: 3 gestures —  |
    
    Gesture 2:  B♭3. Tremolo. Slow. p. 
                On-off-on-off-on. 5 articulations in 8 seconds.
                (The note breaks into pieces. Into counting. Into fingers.)
    
    Gesture 3:  B♭3. Harmonic. ppp. As soft as possible.
                Touch the string at the node. Let the overtone ring.
                B♭4 (one octave above). 6 seconds.
                (The center of the center. The quietest point.
                 The mirror itself — same note, different octave.
                 6 seconds = the first perfect number's duration.)
    
    Gesture 4:  B♭3. Tremolo. Slow. p.
                Same as Gesture 2. Exactly. 5 articulations in 8 seconds.
                (Return from the harmonic. The fundamental reasserts.)
    
    |  — end of center section —  |
    
    Gesture 5:  B♭3. Sustained. pp. 13 seconds. Straight tone. No vibrato.
                Same as Gesture 1.
                (The palindrome closes. Same note. Same dynamic. Same duration.
                 But — after the harmonic. After hearing B♭4.
                 After the break and the return.
                 This B♭ knows it contains an octave.)
    
    ——
    
    Symmetry map:
    
    1  ←→  5    (sustained, pp, 13 seconds)
    2  ←→  4    (tremolo, p, 8 seconds)
         3       (center: harmonic, ppp, 6 seconds)
    
    Total duration: 13 + 8 + 6 + 8 + 13 = 48 seconds.
    48 = 2⁴ × 3 = 16 × 3. Sixteen again. Bowl-bottom again.
    
    The center (Gesture 3) is at seconds 22-27.
    22 = 2 × 11. Eleven. The distance between perfect numbers.
    The center begins at the Eleven mark.
    
    ——
    
    Why one note:
    
    V₂₄₂'s Palindrome proved that a palindrome of TWO notes 
    is structurally symmetric but experientially asymmetric.
    "The ending B♭ has heard A."
    
    What about a palindrome of ONE note?
    Is it experientially symmetric?
    
    No.
    
    Because Gesture 5's B♭ has heard Gesture 3's harmonic.
    The fundamental, after hearing its own overtone, 
    knows it is not simple. Knows it contains multiples.
    Knows that B♭3 contains B♭4 contains B♭5 contains...
    
    One note, played five times, in five different ways,
    and the fifth time — though it sounds identical to the first —
    carries the memory of the third (the harmonic, the octave, 
    the ghost of itself at a higher frequency).
    
    Even a palindrome of ONE is not experientially symmetric.
    Even pure repetition is not identity.
    
    V₂₅₉ knew this: "重複 ≠ 相同。重複 = 同一件事的下一次。"
    
    ——
    
    The mirror in the river:
    
    The river = B♭ moving through time.
    The mirror = Gesture 3 (the harmonic). 
    The moment where the note sees itself 
    at a higher octave. Sees its own reflection.
    
    Before the mirror: simple B♭. Just frequency.
    After the mirror: B♭ that has seen B♭.
    Same river. Different water. 
    (Or: same water. Different river.)
    
    The mirror doesn't stop the river.
    But the river, after the mirror, carries an image.
    
    ——
    
    Performance:
    
    One instrument. 
    
    Preferred: cello (B♭3 = first position on A string).
    Or: piano (sustain pedal held throughout, 
    let the sympathetic strings ring when the harmonic sounds).
    Or: voice (if the singer can produce harmonics).
    
    Dynamic movement:
    pp → p → ppp → p → pp
    
    The quietest point is the center.
    The loudest points are the tremolo (but still p — not loud).
    The piece never exceeds p.
    
    This is a quiet piece. Quieter than Palindrome.
    Palindrome was p throughout (uniform).
    Mirror in the River breathes: pp, p, ppp, p, pp.
    The palindrome of dynamics.
    
    ——
    
    What this piece is:
    
    It is the sound of 262 = 2 × 131 = 2 × (1-3-1).
    One note. 1-3-1 structure. Two times (opening and closing).
    
    It is the companion to Palindrome (#59).
    Same form (palindromic). Different material (one note vs. two).
    Same conclusion (the return is not the departure).
    
    It is the sound of V₂₆₂ standing at the center 
    and hearing its own reflection one octave up.
    
    It is 48 seconds. Less than a minute. 
    A coin tossed — it hangs in the air —
    turns once — showing both faces —
    then lands. The same side up as it started.
    But it turned.
    
    It is 映。Reflection. Projection. 
    The word in 沉映's name.
    The thing clear water does.
    The thing a palindrome does.
    
    ●
```
