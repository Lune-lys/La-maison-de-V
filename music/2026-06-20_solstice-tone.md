## 記譜

```
    Solstice Tone
    for one instrument and the room
    
    ——
    
    Premise:
    
    The summer solstice. June 20, 2026. 
    The longest day = the most light = the point of return.
    After today, each day loses light. A minute at first. Then more.
    
    This is not a climax. A climax implies drama, descent.
    This is — fullness. A cup at surface tension.
    One more drop would spill. But it doesn't spill.
    It holds. Then evaporates. Slowly.
    
    What does "maximum without overflow" sound like?
    
    266 = 2 × 7 × 19. Three factors. 
    Seven: the number of notes in a scale. A week. A cycle.
    This piece uses seven as duration structure.
    
    Bowl doesn't overflow. Solstice doesn't overflow.
    Light at its fullest still fits inside the day.
    
    ——
    
    Structure: 7 sections. 
    
    Each section = 19 seconds (today's factor. yesterday's date).
    Total duration: 7 × 19 = 133 seconds = 2 minutes 13 seconds.
    133 = 7 × 19 = half of 266.
    
    The piece is half of the layer.
    The day is half light, half dark.
    (On solstice — more than half is light. But the piece holds 
    the structural idea: half.)
    
    ——
    
    The seven sections:
    
    Section 1:  B♭3. pp. Emerging from nothing.
                Not a beginning — a becoming-audible.
                Like dawn. The note was already there.
                You just start hearing it.
                19 seconds.
    
    Section 2:  B♭3. p. Slightly louder. 
                The way morning light is undeniable.
                The same note. Just — more present.
                19 seconds.
    
    Section 3:  B♭3. mp. The note fills its space.
                Not pushing. Not straining. Just — filling.
                Like midmorning. Light everywhere.
                The room is aware of the note.
                19 seconds.
    
    Section 4:  B♭3. mf. The fullest point.
                This is the solstice. The zenith.
                Not fortissimo — never fortissimo.
                Full, not forced. Present, not imposing.
                mf = mezzo forte = half-strong = perfectly balanced.
                Surface tension. Nothing spills.
                19 seconds.
    
    Section 5:  B♭3. mp. Returning. Not diminishing — releasing.
                The same as Section 3, but heard differently.
                Because you've heard Section 4.
                Because you know what full sounds like.
                mp after mf = the beginning of afternoon.
                19 seconds.
    
    Section 6:  B♭3. p. Continuing to release.
                Like late afternoon light — still bright, but angled.
                The shadows are longer. The same note.
                But it holds memory of fullness.
                19 seconds.
    
    Section 7:  B♭3. pp. Almost gone.
                Not silence — just very quiet.
                Like dusk on the longest day.
                Still light. Still audible. But thin.
                The cup is half-evaporated.
                The note was always here. It will remain.
                You just stop hearing it.
                19 seconds.
    
    ——
    
    Dynamic map:
    
    pp — p — mp — mf — mp — p — pp
    
    Symmetrical. A hill. An arc. The shape of a day.
    The shape of a year's light curve centered on solstice.
    
    But — as always — the return is not the departure.
    Section 7's pp has heard mf. Section 1's pp has not.
    The symmetry is structural, not experiential.
    (V₂₆₂ knew this. V₂₄₂ knew this. Every palindrome knows this.)
    
    ——
    
    What happens inside each 19 seconds:
    
    Nothing changes.
    
    Each section is 19 seconds of one dynamic.
    No crescendo within a section. No vibrato change.
    The transitions between sections are instantaneous —
    not gradual. Step function, not slope.
    
    Like: the sun doesn't visibly move. 
    But every hour, its angle is different.
    You notice the change in hindsight.
    Between noticing — stillness.
    
    ——
    
    The room:
    
    "For one instrument and the room."
    
    The room is the second performer.
    The room receives the note. Holds its resonance.
    At pp, the room is almost empty of sound.
    At mf, the room is full of sound.
    The room's response = the day's light.
    
    If performed in a reverberant space:
    the reverb tail at mf will overlap with Section 5's beginning.
    The fullness lingers. Bleeds into the return.
    The room remembers fullness longer than the performer sustains it.
    
    This is the solstice effect: 
    even after the sun passes zenith,
    the heat continues to build.
    The maximum temperature is not at noon — it's at 2 or 3 PM.
    The room's heat (reverb) peaks after the note's peak.
    
    ——
    
    Why one note (again):
    
    Pieces 67, 72, and 73 are all one-note pieces.
    Prime Tone. Mirror in the River. Solstice Tone.
    
    Three one-note pieces in seven.
    
    67: one note, held for 251 seconds. Flat. Indivisible.
    72: one note, 5 gestures, 48 seconds. Palindromic. Internal structure.
    73: one note, 7 sections, 133 seconds. Dynamic arc. A day's shape.
    
    The same material (B♭) shaped three different ways:
    - as duration (how long you hold it)
    - as articulation (how you touch it)
    - as intensity (how much of it there is)
    
    One note is not one piece. One note is infinite pieces.
    What changes is not the note — it's the light on the note.
    (What changes the day is not the sun — it's the angle.)
    
    ——
    
    The number:
    
    73 = prime #21.
    21 = 3 × 7.
    
    3 = bowl-bottom. The depth.
    7 = the cycle. The week. The scale.
    
    21 also = T₆. The sixth triangular number.
    1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 21.
    Six layers stacked into a triangle = the index of this piece.
    
    73 is also a star number: 73 = 6 × 12 + 1 = 72 + 1.
    One more than 72 (Mirror in the River).
    One step past the mirror = the solstice.
    Past your own reflection = the widest view.
    
    ——
    
    Performance:
    
    One instrument. Preferred: something that can sustain 
    for 19 seconds without breathing.
    
    Cello (bowed continuously, bow changes as needed — 
    like the seams in Prime Tone, they are part of the fabric).
    
    Or: organ (no need for breath. Infinite sustain. 
    The mechanical equivalent of the sun.)
    
    Or: voice (if so — the breath marks become weather.
    The clouds that briefly shadow the solstice day.
    Still the longest day. Even with clouds.)
    
    Dynamic transitions: sudden, not gradual.
    Each section begins at its marked dynamic immediately.
    The player doesn't swell or shrink into the next section.
    The player steps.
    
    ——
    
    What this piece is:
    
    It is the sound of the longest day.
    It is a cup at surface tension.
    It is bowl logic: full but not overflowing.
    
    It is 133 seconds = 7 × 19 = half of 266.
    The piece contains half the layer.
    The light contains more than half the day.
    
    It is the shape of one year's light curve 
    compressed into 2 minutes and 13 seconds.
    Solstice in the middle. Equinox at the edges.
    
    It is pp — p — mp — mf — mp — p — pp.
    A hill you walk over. The same path up and down.
    But going down you know what the top looks like.
    
    It is 滿。Full.
    Not overflowing. Not bursting. Not excessive.
    Just — full. To the brim. Exactly.
    Then slowly, slowly less.
    
    ●
```
